\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} We plan to evaluate on the same long-document, multimodal QA benchmarks used by MAGE-RAG -- LongDocURL~\citep{longdocurl2024} and MMLongBench-Doc~\citep{mmlongbenchdoc2024} -- plus SimpleQA~\citep{simpleqa2024} as a short-form factuality sanity check that layers of graph pruning do not regress simple, single-fact questions. Table~\ref{tab:baselines} reproduces MAGE-RAG's published numbers on the first two benchmarks as the reference to match or beat; DOM-Graph RAG's own results on these benchmarks are pending (see status paragraph below), so no comparison row is filled in yet.

\paragraph{Planned ablations.} Three ablations isolate each contribution of \S\ref{sec:method}: (a) \emph{tiling}: DOM-guided tiling vs.\ a blind fixed-height pixel grid over the same rendered pages, with the reader model held fixed, to isolate the effect of content-aligned tile boundaries; (b) \emph{evidence control}: the budgeted controller of \S\ref{sec:controller} vs.\ a static top-$k$ retrieval baseline using the same relevance scores, at matched budget/$k$, to isolate the effect of best-first frontier expansion over a fixed ranking; (c) \emph{contrastive fine-tuning}: the base VLM vs.\ the LoRA-fine-tuned reader of \S\ref{sec:contrastive}, to isolate the effect of tile-level contrastive adaptation.

\paragraph{Status.} Phases 1--3 of \S\ref{sec:method} (rendering, tiling, graph construction and export, contrastive fine-tuning pipeline, and evidence controller) are implemented, unit- and smoke-tested, and demonstrated end-to-end on live Wikipedia pages (\S\ref{sec:implementation}). The contrastive dataset of \S\ref{sec:contrastive} was initially exercised on a single page only; we have since started scaling QA-pair generation to a small multi-page corpus with the batch script of \S\ref{sec:implementation} (five pages beyond the original pilot, 62 contrastive examples at the time of writing, growing as the batch continues). Scaling this generation surfaced a practical failure mode worth recording: our local reader (\code{qwen3-vl} via Ollama) reasons at length before answering, and under Ollama's default context window this reasoning occasionally consumed the entire budget before a final answer was emitted, silently producing an empty response that was discarded as an unusable tile rather than flagged as an error. We fixed this by widening the context window and disabling reasoning for this extraction-only step, which also matters for cost: even after the fix, QA generation on local (Apple Silicon, non-datacenter) hardware runs at roughly two minutes per tile, which is itself informative about the compute the benchmarks and ablations below will require at the scale MAGE-RAG's published numbers were obtained on. We will complete this section, with full results tables, in a subsequent revision, most likely after moving generation and fine-tuning to rented GPU compute.
