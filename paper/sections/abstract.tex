Recent visual retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems render documents as pixel screenshots and let a vision-language model (VLM) read tiles of the image directly, avoiding the information loss of OCR and text extraction. PixelRAG~\citep{pixelrag2026} popularized this idea but tiles the page on a fixed pixel grid, blind to content boundaries, so a table or paragraph can be split mid-tile. Separately, MAGE-RAG~\citep{magerag2026} organizes long documents into a multigranular evidence graph and reads it with an online controller that activates, opens, and prunes graph nodes under a fixed budget, but targets already-parsed PDFs rather than rendered web pages. We present \textbf{DOM-Graph RAG}, which replaces PixelRAG's blind grid with tiling guided by the page's own DOM structure, so tile boundaries follow content boundaries by construction. The resulting tiles form a MAGE-RAG-style multigranular graph, extended across pages via hyperlink edges into a small corpus graph. We fine-tune a VLM reader contrastively on synthetic (tile, question) pairs with mined hard negatives (LoRA, InfoNCE), and implement an online evidence controller that evolves every node through a four-state machine (\state{inactive}$\to$\state{active}$\to$\state{opened}/\state{pruned}) via best-first search under budget, giving the reader a small, prioritized evidence subgraph instead of the whole page.

We describe the full, open-sourced pipeline and report three preliminary results. First, on a slice of our own synthetic QA set, DOM-guided tiling's lexical retrieval signal is 15.7--15.8$\times$ stronger than chance, against 3.9$\times$ for a blind fixed-height grid at the same resolution, once pool-size differences between the two tilings are controlled for. Second, on a held-out article-level validation split, the fine-tuned reader nearly doubles Recall@1 over the base model (0.433 $\to$ 0.785, MRR 0.578 $\to$ 0.856) at realistic per-page retrieval scale. Third, on cross-page multi-hop questions built from HotpotQA, the controller's current \code{reading\_order}-only frontier expansion never beats a static top-$k$ baseline over the same scores, turning a limitation already flagged qualitatively into a measured one. Full benchmark evaluation against MAGE-RAG's published LongDocURL and MMLongBench-Doc numbers is ongoing work, reported as such in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
