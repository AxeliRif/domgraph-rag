Recent visual retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems render documents as pixel screenshots and let a vision-language model (VLM) read tiles of the image directly, avoiding the information loss of OCR and text extraction. PixelRAG~\citep{pixelrag2026} popularized this idea but tiles the rendered page on a fixed pixel grid, blind to content boundaries: a table or paragraph can be sliced in half mid-tile. Separately, MAGE-RAG~\citep{magerag2026} organizes long documents into a multigranular page/element evidence graph and reads it with an online controller that activates, opens, and prunes graph nodes under a fixed action budget, but targets already-parsed long PDFs rather than rendered web pages. We present \textbf{DOM-Graph RAG}, a pipeline that replaces PixelRAG's blind grid with tiling guided by the page's own DOM structure, so tile boundaries follow content boundaries by construction. The resulting tiles are organized into a MAGE-RAG-style multigranular graph, extended across pages via hyperlink edges into a small corpus graph. We fine-tune a VLM reader contrastively on synthetically generated (tile, question) pairs with mined hard negatives (LoRA, both vision tower and decoder, InfoNCE), and implement an online evidence controller that evolves every node through a four-state machine (\state{inactive}$\to$\state{active}$\to$\state{opened}/\state{pruned}) via best-first search under budget, so the reader gets a small, prioritized evidence subgraph instead of the whole page. We describe the full, open-sourced pipeline in detail and report two preliminary results: on a slice of our own synthetic QA set, DOM-guided tiling's lexical retrieval signal is 15.7--15.8$\times$ stronger than chance, against 3.9$\times$ for a blind fixed-height grid at the same resolution, once pool-size differences between the two tilings are controlled for; and, on a held-out article-level validation split, the fine-tuned reader nearly doubles Recall@1 over the base model (0.433 $\to$ 0.785, MRR 0.578 $\to$ 0.856) at realistic per-page retrieval scale. Full benchmark evaluation against MAGE-RAG's published LongDocURL and MMLongBench-Doc numbers is ongoing work, reported as such in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
