\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Text-based document RAG.} Standard RAG pipelines chunk extracted text (fixed-length windows, sentence or section boundaries) and retrieve chunks with a dense bi-encoder~\citep{dpr2020}. Applied to visually rich documents -- PDFs with tables and figures, web pages with infoboxes -- this requires a layout-aware parser or OCR step, both of which are lossy and error-prone on complex layouts, and it discards the two-dimensional structure of the page (which text belongs to which cell, which figure a caption refers to) before retrieval ever runs.

\paragraph{Screenshot-based visual RAG.} PixelRAG~\citep{pixelrag2026} sidesteps text extraction by rendering each page to a fixed-width screenshot and retrieving directly over image tiles with a VLM embedding model (\texttt{Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B} in the reference implementation), following a broader trend of using VLMs as universal, modality-agnostic retrievers. Its tiling strategy is a blind, fixed-height pixel grid: simple and renderer-agnostic, but indifferent to where an element actually begins or ends on the page, so a table or paragraph can straddle a tile boundary. Our tiling procedure (\S\ref{sec:tiling}) keeps PixelRAG's core premise -- a VLM reads image tiles rather than extracted text -- but replaces the grid with the page's own DOM geometry. PixelRAG separately reports image resolution as an efficiency lever, citing up to a $3\times$ token-cost reduction at lower resolutions with accuracy maintained, but specifies neither the resampling filter nor the resolutions tested in the paper or its public repository; rather than accept the claim as reported, we build a matched full-/reduced-resolution tile pair for our own dataset (\S\ref{sec:contrastive}) to measure the tradeoff directly under our own reader and tiling procedure.

\paragraph{Graph-structured evidence and online controllers for long-document QA.} MAGE-RAG~\citep{magerag2026} targets long-document, multimodal QA (evaluated on LongDocURL~\citep{longdocurl2024} and MMLongBench-Doc~\citep{mmlongbenchdoc2024}) by building an offline multigranular evidence graph -- page and element nodes linked by containment, reading order, layout adjacency, section hierarchy, and semantic-neighbor relations -- and reading it online with a budgeted controller (its Algorithm~1) that activates promising nodes, opens a bounded number of them, and prunes the rest, rather than retrieving a static top-$k$ set once. This graph-and-controller design is the direct ancestor of our evidence controller (\S\ref{sec:controller}); the key difference is where the graph comes from: MAGE-RAG assumes an already-parsed document, whereas we construct the graph directly from the DOM of a rendered web page, which yields exact element boundaries and reading order for free but only two of MAGE-RAG's five relation types so far (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Contrastive fine-tuning and hard-negative mining.} Training a bi-encoder retriever with in-batch negatives plus a handful of mined \emph{hard} negatives -- passages that are lexically or semantically close to the query but do not answer it -- is standard practice in dense retrieval, e.g.\ DPR's BM25-mined negatives~\citep{dpr2020}. We adopt the same idea at tile granularity, using TF-IDF cosine similarity in place of BM25 for simplicity, and generate the underlying (question, positive passage) training pairs synthetically by prompting a VLM to read each tile and produce a self-contained question, in the spirit of synthetic query generation for retriever training such as InPars~\citep{inpars2022} and Promptagator~\citep{promptagator2022}, adapted here to image tiles rather than text passages. We fine-tune with LoRA~\citep{lora2021} adapters applied to both the language decoder and the vision tower of the reader VLM, following the general recipe for parameter-efficient adaptation of large pretrained transformers.

\paragraph{Factuality benchmarks.} SimpleQA~\citep{simpleqa2024} measures short-form factual accuracy of language models on simple, unambiguous questions; we include it in our evaluation protocol (\S\ref{sec:experiments}) as a sanity check that evidence-controlled reading does not regress on questions that do not require multi-hop or layout reasoning.
