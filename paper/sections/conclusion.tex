\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

DOM-Graph RAG grounds two ideas in visual and graph-structured RAG -- PixelRAG's screenshot tiles and MAGE-RAG's budgeted graph controller -- in the DOM a rendered web page already provides for free: content-aligned tiling replaces a blind pixel grid, a multigranular graph replaces a flat tile list, a contrastively fine-tuned reader replaces a generic VLM, and a four-state evidence controller replaces reading the whole page or a static top-$k$. Every stage runs end-to-end on live pages. Three preliminary results (\S\ref{sec:experiments}) support this design: DOM-guided tiling carries a real retrieval advantage over a blind grid once pool-size effects are controlled for; the fine-tuned reader substantially outperforms the base model at realistic per-page retrieval; and, on genuinely multi-hop questions, the controller's current \code{reading\_order}-only expansion cannot yet beat a static top-$k$ baseline -- a measured version of a limitation \S\ref{sec:limitations} already flagged, not a new one. Next: an HTML-native benchmark, the resolution ablation, and closing the gaps of \S\ref{sec:limitations} -- most importantly, type-aware frontier expansion beyond \code{reading\_order} and wiring the controller's relevance scoring to the now-validated reader in place of the TF-IDF placeholder.
