\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

DOM-Graph RAG grounds two ideas in visual and graph-structured RAG -- PixelRAG's screenshot tiles and MAGE-RAG's budgeted graph controller -- in the DOM a rendered web page already provides for free: content-aligned tiling replaces a blind pixel grid, a multigranular graph replaces a flat tile list, a contrastively fine-tuned reader replaces a generic VLM, and a four-state evidence controller replaces reading the whole page or a static top-$k$. Every stage runs end-to-end on live pages: a first lexical ablation (\S\ref{sec:experiments}) suggests the tiling carries a real retrieval advantage once pool-size effects are controlled for, and a held-out evaluation of the fine-tuned reader shows it substantially outperforms the base model at realistic per-page retrieval. Next: an HTML-native benchmark, the resolution ablation, and closing the gaps of \S\ref{sec:limitations} -- most importantly, wiring the controller's relevance scoring to this now-validated reader in place of the TF-IDF placeholder.
