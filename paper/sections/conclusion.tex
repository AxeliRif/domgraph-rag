\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We described DOM-Graph RAG, a pipeline that grounds two recent ideas in visual and graph-structured RAG -- PixelRAG's screenshot tiles and MAGE-RAG's budgeted graph controller -- in the one source of structure a rendered web page already provides for free: its DOM. DOM-guided adaptive tiling replaces a blind pixel grid with content-aligned tiles; a multigranular page/element graph, extendable across hyperlinked pages, replaces a flat tile list; a contrastively fine-tuned VLM reader replaces a generic off-the-shelf one; and a four-state, budget-bounded evidence controller replaces retrieving either the whole page or a static top-$k$. Every stage is implemented, tested, and demonstrated end-to-end on live pages. The immediate next step is Phase 4: running the LongDocURL, MMLongBench-Doc, and SimpleQA evaluation and the three ablations laid out in \S\ref{sec:experiments}, and, in parallel, closing the gaps noted in \S\ref{sec:limitations} -- most importantly, replacing the controller's lexical relevance scoring with the fine-tuned reader's own learned similarity once it is trained at scale.
